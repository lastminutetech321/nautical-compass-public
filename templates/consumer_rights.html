Consumer Rights

Federal consumer protection statutes: FCRA, FDCPA, EFTA, and TILA.

Legal Disclaimer: This tool provides educational information about consumer protection law. It does not constitute legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney for case-specific guidance.

FCRA — Fair Credit Reporting Act

15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq. Governs collection, dissemination, and use of consumer credit information.

  • Right to access your credit report (free annually)
  • Right to dispute inaccurate or incomplete information
  • Consumer reporting agencies must investigate disputes within 30 days
  • Furnishers must correct or delete inaccurate data
  • Willful violations: actual damages, statutory damages ($100–$1,000 per violation), punitive damages, attorney fees
  • Negligent violations: actual damages and attorney fees

FDCPA — Fair Debt Collection Practices Act

15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq. Prohibits abusive, unfair, or deceptive practices by debt collectors.

  • Debt collectors may not contact you at inconvenient times or places
  • Right to demand verification of debt (30-day dispute window)
  • Prohibition on harassment, false statements, and unfair practices
  • Right to demand cessation of contact in writing
  • Statutory damages up to $1,000 per action, plus actual damages and attorney fees

EFTA — Electronic Fund Transfer Act

15 U.S.C. § 1693 et seq. Establishes rights and responsibilities for electronic fund transfers.

  • Right to error resolution within 60 days of statement
  • Provisional credit during investigation of disputes
  • Limitation of liability for unauthorized transfers (timely reporting required)
  • Financial institution must complete investigation within 10 business days
  • Violations: actual damages, statutory damages ($100–$1,000 per action), attorney fees

TILA — Truth in Lending Act

15 U.S.C. § 1601 et seq. Requires clear disclosure of loan terms and costs to consumers.

  • Lenders must disclose APR, finance charges, and total repayment amount
  • Right of rescission: 3-day window on certain home-secured loans
  • Billing dispute procedures for open-end credit
  • Statutory damages: $100–$1,000 per closed-end transaction; up to $500,000 class action

Federal Question Jurisdiction

All four statutes create private rights of action enforceable in federal court under 28 U.S.C. § 1331. Attorney fees are available in most consumer statute actions, making representation accessible even for lower-dollar claims.
