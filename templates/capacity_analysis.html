Real party in interest and capacity to sue under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 17.

Rule 17 — Real Party in Interest

An action must be prosecuted in the name of the real party in interest. The court must identify who has the legal right being enforced.

  • Individual bringing their own claim: usually the real party
  • Assignee, subrogee, trustee: substitute as real party
  • Business entities must sue in their proper legal name

Personal Capacity

A personal-capacity suit seeks to impose personal liability on an individual government official for actions taken under color of law.

  • Plaintiff seeks damages from the official personally
  • Qualified immunity may apply
  • Judgment is against the person, not the office

Official Capacity

An official-capacity suit is effectively a suit against the government entity itself.

  • Used when seeking injunctive or declaratory relief
  • Named defendant is the title-holder of the office
  • Damages from the government entity, not the individual
  • Eleventh Amendment immunity may apply for state defendants

Organizational Capacity

A corporation, LLC, or other entity has capacity to sue and be sued in its own name in federal court.

  • Corporate plaintiff: verify standing in home state
  • Unincorporated association: capacity under state law
  • Dissolved entities may lack capacity
