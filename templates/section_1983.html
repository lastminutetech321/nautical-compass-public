42 U.S.C. § 1983 — Civil Rights Navigator

Civil action for deprivation of rights under color of state law.

Legal Disclaimer: This tool provides educational information about § 1983 doctrine. It does not constitute legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney for case-specific guidance.

Statutory Text

"Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage, of any State or Territory or the District of Columbia, subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States or other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured..." — 42 U.S.C. § 1983

Elements Required

  1. Defendant acted under color of state law
  2. Defendant's conduct deprived plaintiff of a right secured by the Constitution or federal law

Private parties generally cannot be sued under § 1983 unless they acted jointly with state actors.

Personal Capacity Claims

Sue the individual official for money damages. The official may raise qualified immunity — shielding liability unless the right was clearly established at the time.

  • Money damages from the officer personally
  • Qualified immunity defense available
  • Must show clearly established constitutional right violated

Official Capacity Claims

Effectively a suit against the government entity. Monell v. Dep't of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978) — municipalities and local governments are "persons" under § 1983.

  • Seek injunctive or declaratory relief
  • Monetary relief against the entity (not Eleventh Amendment barred for local gov't)
  • Must show unconstitutional policy, custom, or failure to train

Injunctive & Declaratory Relief

Courts may issue prospective relief to stop ongoing constitutional violations.

  • Injunction: court order requiring or prohibiting conduct
  • Declaratory judgment: court declares the parties' legal rights
  • State sovereign immunity does not bar prospective relief against state officials — Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908)

Federal Jurisdiction

§ 1983 claims arise under federal law, conferring jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (federal question) and 28 U.S.C. § 1343 (civil rights).

Common § 1983 Violations

  • Fourth Amendment: unlawful search, seizure, or excessive force
  • Fourteenth Amendment: due process, equal protection
  • First Amendment: retaliation for protected speech
  • Eighth Amendment: conditions of confinement
