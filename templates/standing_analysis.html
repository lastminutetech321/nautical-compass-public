Standing Analysis

Three-part constitutional standing under Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992).

Legal Disclaimer: This tool provides educational information about standing doctrine. It does not constitute legal advice. Consult a licensed attorney for case-specific guidance.

1. Injury in Fact

The plaintiff must allege a concrete, particularized, and actual or imminent injury — not speculative or hypothetical.

  • Concrete: real, not abstract
  • Particularized: affects the plaintiff personally
  • Actual or imminent: not conjectural

Financial loss, unpaid wages, property damage, and credit injury are common qualifying injuries.

2. Causation (Traceability)

The injury must be fairly traceable to the challenged conduct of the defendant — not the result of an independent third party.

  • Direct causal connection required
  • Intervening independent causes can defeat standing
  • Identify the specific actor and specific conduct

3. Redressability

It must be likely — not merely speculative — that a favorable court decision will redress the injury.

  • Damages: monetary award must address the loss
  • Injunction: court order must be able to stop the harm
  • Declaratory relief: must resolve a live controversy

Federal Jurisdiction Hook

Where a federal statute creates the right or the defendant is a government actor, standing may anchor federal question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331.
